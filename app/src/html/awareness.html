Awareness

Organ Donation

Organ donation means giving a part of the body to a person suffering from end-stage organ disease who needs a transplant. Organs that can be donated include the kidney, liver, heart, lungs, small bowel, and tissues such as corneas, heart valves, skin, and bones.

Legal Framework in India

Organ donation in India is regulated by the Transplantation of Human Organs and Tissues Act, 1994. The law allows both deceased and living donors to donate their organs, and identifies brain death as a form of death.

Types of Donors

  • Living Donors: Individuals who donate one of their kidneys, a portion of their pancreas, or part of their liver.
  • Deceased Donors: Individuals who donate after brain death or cardiac death.

Green Corridors

Green corridors are designated routes used for the quick transport of organs to ensure successful transplantation. This system helps reduce the time between organ retrieval and transplant.

Facts About Organ Donation

  • Anybody can be an organ donor regardless of age, caste, religion, or community.
  • There is no defined age for donating organs.
  • Tissues like cornea and heart valves can be donated in case of natural death, but vital organs like heart and liver can only be donated in case of brain death.
  • Organ donation can save multiple lives.

How Organ Donation Works

  1. Sign-Up: Individuals register as organ donors.
  2. Medical Evaluation: If a person is critically ill, doctors assess if organ donation is viable.
  3. Consent & Matching: Consent is obtained, and a match is found using databases.
  4. Organ Recovery: Skilled surgeons recover the organs for transplant.
  5. Transport & Transplant: The organs are transported and transplanted into recipients.
